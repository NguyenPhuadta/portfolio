Context

V9Snap is an Android photo editor. Its core experience does not end when a user finishes editing—the journey only creates value when the user successfully saves the result.

Monetization happens downstream and near the end of this journey, which made the design problem both a usability and business question.

The product problem

Friction in the edit-to-save journey reduced completion. Simplifying one step without understanding what followed could improve a local interaction while weakening progression toward monetization.

The design goal was not simply to make saving easier. It was to improve completion while preserving the logic of the full funnel.

Following the funnel

I worked with Product and Engineering to look at the journey as a connected sequence: editing intent, save action, downstream steps, and monetization exposure.

Design direction

The team streamlined the path between completing an edit and successfully saving the result. The final portfolio version will document the specific screen-level changes after approved artifacts are provided.

  • Clarify the primary action at the end of editing.
  • Reduce avoidable friction before save completion.
  • Preserve meaningful monetization opportunities downstream.
  • Use consistent tracking across shared actions and contexts.

Measurement

The project also involved tracking conventions for shared behaviors and monetization-related events. Context was distinguished with parameters where appropriate rather than forcing every shared action into a screen-specific event.

Team outcome

These results reflect team improvements to the journey and downstream product performance.

Learning

A flow cannot be evaluated only by the ease of its next tap. Product design decisions become stronger when the team follows behavior far enough to understand how an upstream UX change affects a downstream business outcome.
