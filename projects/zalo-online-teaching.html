Student project · UXFoundation K22

Helping online teachers keep classes engaging

An early product concept for Zalo that explored how online teachers could spend less time preparing lessons, see participation more clearly, and keep a class moving toward a shared goal.

Concept project

This is a student project, not a launched Zalo feature.

01 · The challenge

Online teaching made engagement harder to see

Desk research and teacher interviews pointed to a recurring challenge: online classes could become quiet and less interactive than in-person ones. Teachers had less direct feedback from students, had to manage several tools, and could spend significant time preparing warm-up activities and lessons.

The opportunity was to help teachers create more engaging lessons while reducing preparation effort.

02 · Concept direction

Support the class before and during the lesson

The concept addressed three moments in an online teaching workflow: preparing a lesson, noticing participation during class, and giving the class a shared reason to stay involved.

03 · Lesson preparation

Start with a lesson template, then make it your own

Teachers enter information about an upcoming lesson in Zalo. The proposed concept connects with template sources such as Canva and Slidesgo, using AI to find or generate a suitable lesson template. The teacher can then edit it and save it back to Zalo.

04 · Participation

See who is taking part—and who may need a prompt

An activity dashboard shows the number of students in class and the number who have interacted. Teachers can rank students by live interaction and send a request to students near the bottom of the list, asking them to turn on their microphone and camera to respond.

05 · Shared goal

Give the class a goal to work toward together

Before the lesson, a teacher sets a class target, such as a number of speaking contributions or correct answers. A progress bar shows the class’s progress in real time; completing the target unlocks a reward set by the teacher.

06 · Learning

Designing for preparation and participation together

The concept connected two parts of the teaching experience: helping teachers prepare engaging material efficiently, then giving them clearer signals and prompts to keep participation active during the lesson.
