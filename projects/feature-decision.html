Should we really kill this feature?

Using data to slow down a premature removal decision and separate low value from low exposure.

FormatProduct mini-case
FocusDecision quality
MethodBehavioral analysis
Feature adoptionInvestigate

Low usage ≠ low value

The assumption

The team was moving quickly toward removing a feature because its performance appeared weak. That conclusion risked treating the observed metric as the cause rather than a signal that needed more context.

The question behind the metric

Was the feature underperforming because users did not value it—or because too few users could discover, understand, or reach it?

Instead of accepting the initial assumption, I used behavioral analysis to challenge the framing and encourage a more complete investigation before making an irreversible product decision.

Analysis framework

The analysis should distinguish several different conditions. Only dimensions supported by final project evidence will be published:

  • Exposure: how many eligible users encountered the feature?
  • Discoverability: could users understand where to find it?
  • Adoption: what happened after meaningful exposure?
  • Repeat usage: did users return after the first use?
  • Funnel and cohort context: were certain groups behaving differently?
Evidence placeholder
Exposure → discovery → adoption → repeat usage

Decision principle

A low aggregate number cannot tell the team whether a feature is inherently weak, poorly exposed, badly positioned, or measured in the wrong context. The decision needed evidence that could distinguish those possibilities.

Why this case matters

This mini-case is less about defending one feature and more about product judgment: slowing down when a metric is being interpreted too confidently, asking what the data can and cannot prove, and making a decision that is easier to explain and reverse.

Evidence still needed

The public version intentionally stops short of claiming a final outcome. Before publication, it needs the approved feature context, analysis screenshots, dimensions actually used, and the resulting team decision.
